In Charlotte, subsurface investigation addresses the complex residual soils and partially weathered rock of the Piedmont physiographic province, where saprolitic profiles can mask variable bearing strata. Local practice follows IBC Chapter 18 and NCDOT guidelines, emphasizing targeted exploration to delineate the transition from stiff silty clays to competent bedrock. In-situ methods such as cone penetration testing provide continuous stratigraphic profiling, while standard penetration testing remains the benchmark for disturbed sampling and correlating soil consistency across the Mecklenburg County area.

These investigations underpin foundation design for mid-rise commercial structures, warehouse developments, and infrastructure projects where differential settlement is a key concern. The saprolite mantle demands careful interpretation of penetration resistance to avoid overestimating end-bearing capacity. Integrating piezocone soundings with conventional borings refines the groundwater and strength model, ensuring safe, code-compliant recommendations for shallow and deep foundation alternatives.

Geotechnical investigation in Charlotte, North Carolina, is a critical first step for any construction project, designed to characterize subsurface conditions and mitigate risks associated with the region's complex geology. The city straddles the Piedmont physiographic province, where deep residual soils derived from the weathering of igneous and metamorphic bedrock, such as granite and diorite, are prevalent. These silty sands and sandy silts, locally known as "saprolite," can exhibit variable strength and drainage characteristics, often with a transition to partially weathered rock (PWR) at depth. A robust investigation, guided by the International Building Code (IBC) as adopted by Mecklenburg County, is non-negotiable for foundation design, especially given the presence of expansive clays in certain deposits. Our comprehensive approach begins with a desktop study and is anchored by advanced In-Situ techniques to deliver accurate, site-specific data.

Field exploration methodology in Charlotte adheres strictly to standards set by ASTM International, ensuring data integrity and repeatability. A typical investigation combines destructive and non-destructive methods, starting with Standard Penetration Tests (SPT) per ASTM D1586, which provide disturbed samples and a crucial "N-value" for empirical analysis. This is powerfully supplemented with Cone Penetration Testing (CPT), performed to ASTM D5778, which delivers a near-continuous profile of tip resistance, sleeve friction, and pore pressure. This pairing is exceptionally effective for identifying soft zones, evaluating liquefaction potential, and assessing the interface between residual soil and bedrock. For direct measurements of soil stiffness and deformation characteristics, we deploy specialized methods like the Flat Dilatometer Test (DMT) and the Ménard pressuremeter test (PMT), which are essential for settlement-sensitive designs on Piedmont soils.

The diverse development landscape of Charlotte, from high-rise towers in Uptown to sprawling infrastructure in fast-growing suburbs like Ballantyne, demands tailored investigation strategies. For deep foundation design of skyscrapers, determining the top of competent rock and its integrity is paramount, often requiring a combination of SPT refusal data and CPT refusal criteria. In contrast, warehouse and pavement projects on shallow foundations rely heavily on field density tests using the sand cone method during construction, but the pre-design phase focuses on bearing capacity and settlement. Slope stability analyses for roadway cuts or residential developments on the city's rolling terrain require a precise understanding of shear strength parameters, which can be accurately derived from CPT and DMT data. Every investigation is scoped to address the specific geotechnical hazards of the Piedmont, ensuring that foundation recommendations are both safe and economical.

A phased investigation process begins with a site walkover and utility clearance, followed by the mobilization of specialized drilling and testing equipment. Our field data is integrated into a comprehensive factual and interpretative report, moving beyond raw logs to provide actionable engineering parameters. Deliverables include detailed boring and sounding logs, a geological site model, and clear recommendations for foundation type (shallow vs. deep), allowable bearing pressures, lateral earth pressures, and seismic site class. The ultimate value lies in de-risking the project; by precisely defining the challenging subsurface profile of the Charlotte region through a combination of Standard Penetration Testing and advanced in-situ methods, we enable structural engineers and contractors to proceed with confidence, avoiding costly overruns and post-construction failures from unforeseen ground conditions.
